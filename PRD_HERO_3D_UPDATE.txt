================================================================================
PRD ADDENDUM — INTRO STORYBOARD (LOADING EXPERIENCE) & VISUAL DEPTH UPDATE
================================================================================

PARENT DOCUMENT:
This addendum extends MASTER_PRD.txt (3D Immersive Developer Portfolio).
All rules, design principles, and constraints in MASTER_PRD.txt still apply.
This document does NOT replace it — it REPLACES the placeholder concept in
Section 33 (Loading Experience) with the detailed storyboard defined below,
and adds detail to Section 20 (Visual Design Direction). It also relates to,
but is distinct from, Section 6 (3D / Immersive Visual Direction) — see
"ARCHITECTURE & PLACEMENT" below for the important distinction.

CONTEXT:
Current implementation (About + Skills sections) is functional but has two
gaps identified during review:
1. No intro/loading sequence exists yet — the site currently jumps straight
   to content with no entrance moment.
2. The visual surface is flat and overly dominated by a single black tone,
   lacking depth and a sense of "life".

This addendum defines a concrete full-screen intro storyboard sequence and
a set of depth/color adjustments to close both gaps. Work on this addendum
may proceed in parallel with, or after, existing phases — but must not
break what is already working in About/Skills.

IMPORTANT:
Read this entire document before implementing.
Do not implement this addendum before Phase 1 (Foundation) of MASTER_PRD.txt
is complete, since it depends on the global layout, color tokens, and
navigation already being in place.
Do not skip straight to advanced polish (Section 4 of this addendum) before
the storyboard sequence itself (Section 1) is working end-to-end.

================================================================================
1. INTRO STORYBOARD SEQUENCE (FULL-SCREEN LOADING EXPERIENCE)
================================================================================

Goal: tell a literal, relatable micro-story — the site itself appears to
fail to load, breaks down into visible broken code, then the developer's
identity is revealed as the resolution, and the real site loads in. This
is more concrete than a purely abstract motion/color metaphor, so it needs
one explicit safety rule (below) to avoid reading as a real technical
failure.

SAFETY RULE — DO NOT MIMIC A REAL BROWSER/OS ERROR:
The "failure" must always read as a stylized in-scene event, never as an
actual browser or OS error. Do NOT reproduce real browser chrome, real
"This site can't be reached" / DNS-error / crash-screen styling, or
anything that could make a visitor think their connection or the site
itself is genuinely broken. Keep the failure expressed entirely within
this project's own dark/code visual language (the 3D panels, syntax
colors, glitch effects already defined elsewhere in this PRD) so it is
unambiguously part of an intentional scene, not a real error state. Avoid
rapid high-contrast flashing during the glitch moment (Beat B) for
photosensitivity safety — keep any glitch effect brief and restrained.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
ARCHITECTURE & PLACEMENT — READ BEFORE IMPLEMENTING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
This sequence is NOT part of the Hero section's own 3D canvas, and it does
NOT play "inside" the page while the visitor is browsing. It is a
full-screen, fixed-position INTRO OVERLAY that covers the entire viewport
(z-index above all page content) and plays BEFORE the website itself is
revealed — this is the site's loading/entrance experience, replacing the
simple progress-bar placeholder in MASTER_PRD.txt Section 33.

Sequence of events:
1. Visitor loads or refreshes the page.
2. The full-screen intro overlay renders immediately, on top of everything.
3. Scenes 1 → 2 → 3 (defined below) play once, full-screen.
4. At the end of Scene 3, the overlay fades/dissolves out and is removed
   from the DOM (or hidden), REVEALING the actual homepage underneath.
5. The homepage's Hero section is shown already in its normal resting
   state (per MASTER_PRD.txt Section 6 — mouse parallax, scroll-linked
   movement, etc.) — the storyboard does NOT replay again inside the Hero
   itself. The Hero's own 3D scene (Section 6) is a separate, ongoing
   experience that exists only AFTER the intro has finished.

Frequency: this intro plays on EVERY page load and every refresh — it is
NOT gated behind sessionStorage/localStorage/cookies. Every visit shows
the full sequence again, by explicit product decision.

Note: because this now doubles as the loading experience, tie the scene
transitions loosely to actual asset/JS readiness where practical (e.g. do
not dissolve into the homepage before critical Hero/fonts/assets are
ready) — but do not block or artificially extend the ~8s sequence if
assets load faster; see Timing & Behavior Rules below.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEAT A — "The Glimpse"   (approx. 0s – 1.2s)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Visual:
* A brief, dim/low-opacity glimpse of the real homepage attempting to
  render: the actual nav bar and a rough silhouette of the Hero layout
  appear faintly, as if the page is normally starting to load.
* No 3D panels yet — this beat is short and intentionally underwhelming,
  a false start.

Purpose: sell the premise that "this is the site loading" before it
visibly fails, so Beat B reads as a disruption of something real rather
than an abstract animation starting cold.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEAT B — "The Crash"   (approx. 1.2s – 3s)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Visual:
* The glimpsed layout fractures — a brief, restrained glitch/chromatic-
  aberration moment (see SAFETY RULE above: no rapid flashing) — and
  breaks apart into ONE hero code panel, large, centered/front-of-frame,
  in sharp focus. Do not scatter 4-5 similarly-sized panels; at most one
  additional panel may exist, smaller, behind the hero panel with a soft
  depth-of-field blur, purely for depth. Ambient decorative geometry
  (floating diamonds/octahedra) stays minimal — one dim instance at most.
* Do NOT show the resolved "LIFECYCLE PIPELINE / CODE-BUILD-ITERATE-SHIP"
  panel here — that belongs only to Beat D. Showing it now spoils the
  resolution before the problem is established.

HERO PANEL CONTENT (concrete copy — use this or a close variant, not
generic/abstract filler text):

REVISED — the bug is now a multi-line, cascading problem rather than a
single broken line. A single-line highlight made the eventual "fixed"
payoff in Beat D feel low-stakes/low-value. A problem that visibly touches
several related lines reads as more substantial, so the resolution feels
more earned.

  Filename label: build.pipeline.ts

  Code:
    async function deployRelease() {
      const artifact = await pipeline.run();
      const signature = await sign(artifact.output.hash);
      const target = await resolveEdgeRegion(artifact.meta.region);
      return deploy(artifact, signature, target);
    }

  Visual diagnostic markers (no literal words like "Error"/"Bug"/"Failed"):
  * THREE lines get the solid full-line red highlight treatment (not
    just one): `const signature = await sign(artifact.output.hash);`,
    `const target = await resolveEdgeRegion(artifact.meta.region);`, and
    `return deploy(artifact, signature, target);` — this reads as a
    cascading failure (one bad value flowing downstream into two more
    broken calls), which is more visually substantial than a single flat
    line, without needing any literal "Error" text.
  * A small solid red dot in the left gutter next to each of those three
    lines, matching the IDE error-indicator convention.
  * A faint connecting line/bracket in the gutter linking the three
    flagged lines top-to-bottom is optional (nice-to-have, not required)
    to visually reinforce "these are related", but do not let it add
    visual clutter — a plain gutter dot on each line is sufficient if the
    connector proves fiddly to implement well.
  * The first line (`const artifact = await pipeline.run();`) and the
    closing brace stay in the normal syntax palette — do not tint the
    whole panel red; the red signal stays localized to the three related
    broken lines, but together they must read as an obvious, connected
    problem, not three isolated unrelated errors.

  In Beat D, all three lines transition together from red to the green
  "fixed" highlight (see Beat D FIX ANIMATION below) — the multi-line
  problem resolving together is what makes the payoff feel substantial.

  This filename and function deliberately echo the "Code → Build → Iterate
  → Ship" pipeline shown resolved in Beat D — the hero panel is an early,
  broken version of the same idea, giving the sequence narrative
  continuity (same subject, before vs. after).

Camera & lighting:
* Subtle camera micro-jitter/instability (small, restrained — not
  violent), focused on the hero panel.
* Lighting: slightly cooler/dimmer than the resting state, with the hero
  panel itself still clearly legible.

Purpose: establish tension through ONE clear, readable, authentic signal
(a real broken line of code) rather than through visual clutter or
literal error messaging.

CAPTION COPY (new — approved wording, use verbatim):
Once the crash/fracture has settled and the hero panel with its red
highlight is visible, fade in a short caption near/below the panel:

    "Every build breaks somewhere."

Keep it small, restrained typography (not competing with the code panel
itself), fades in shortly after the panel settles, and fades out as
Beat C begins. This is the ONLY new copy in Beat B — do not add any other
text.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEAT C — "The Reveal"   (approx. 3s – 6s — the emotional payoff; give it
room, do not rush this beat)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
CRITICAL: this beat must NOT render as empty/blank space with the panel
just sitting there — the copy reveal IS the content of this beat and must
be clearly, deliberately animated on screen. An implementation where this
beat feels "bland" or empty (no visible text motion) is incorrect.

Visual:
* The full-line red highlight and gutter dot on the hero panel resolve
  away first (the highlight shrinks/fades, the specific "broken line"
  signal disappears/corrects).
* Out of this resolution, the identity/role copy reveals — reuse the
  EXISTING Hero name/title from MASTER_PRD.txt Section 5 verbatim (no
  eyebrow — "PORTFOLIO / 2026" has been removed from the Hero per Section
  5's revision and must NOT appear here either, for consistency), PLUS
  one new approved personal line (replacing the standard tagline for this
  intro moment only — the standard tagline still appears normally in the
  persistent Hero section once the overlay dissolves, so it is not lost,
  just not repeated twice). Give the whole stack a deliberate staggered
  reveal so the beat has presence:
    [YOUR NAME]                         (fades + slides up first,
                                          largest, with a soft accent-
                                          color glow behind/around it)
    FULL-STACK DEVELOPER                (fades + slides up, ~0.15-0.2s
                                          after the name)
    "I don't just design interfaces —   (fades + slides up last, ~0.15-
    I fix what's underneath."            0.2s after the title; may use a
                                          subtle typewriter/character-
                                          reveal on this line specifically
                                          for extra presence)
  Each line should use a short stagger (roughly 0.15-0.25s offset between
  lines), not all lines appearing simultaneously — this staggered timing
  is what gives the beat life instead of feeling flat.
  This new personal line is the ONLY new copy in Beat C beyond the
  existing Section 5 name/title — do not introduce further text, and do
  not add any eyebrow/status label (see MASTER_PRD.txt Section 5 revision
  — no "SYSTEM: ONLINE" or similar invented badges anywhere).
* Any remaining red/amber tint fades out and transitions into the accent
  color (#8AB4FF) via a smooth color interpolation, not a hard cut.
* Camera stabilizes; jitter reduces to zero.
* Hold the fully-revealed copy on screen for a brief beat (~0.5-0.8s)
  before Beat D begins, so the viewer actually has time to read it — do
  not cut away the instant the last line finishes animating in.

Purpose: this beat IS the "who's behind this" moment — resolved through a
calm visual shift (chaos → order, warning tone → accent tone) paired with
the developer's actual name/title, given real presence through staggered
motion and a touch of glow — not through any "let me help you"
service-style copy, and not through static, motionless text either.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEAT D — "The Load"   (approx. 6s – 8s)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Visual:
* FIX ANIMATION (new): the hero panel's three broken lines, which no
  longer carry the red full-line highlight (resolved at the start of
  Beat C), now play their resolution payoff — a GREEN full-line highlight
  sweeps/fades in across all three lines in quick succession (top to
  bottom, ~0.1-0.15s apart — same treatment as the Beat B red highlight,
  but green, using a success-toned green consistent with the overall
  palette, not a jarring neon green), clearly signaling "fixed/working"
  before fading out as the panel transitions into the Section 6
  composition. This is a brief beat-opening moment (~0.6-1s total for all
  three), not a lingering effect.
* CLOSING CAPTION COPY (new — approved wording, use verbatim), appearing
  briefly alongside or just after the green-highlight moment, then fading
  out before the overlay fully dissolves:

      "This is what I do — turn broken into shipped."

  This is the ONLY new copy in Beat D — do not add further text. Keep it
  brief on screen (~1-1.5s) so it doesn't delay the dissolve.
* The additional panels/nodes needed for the full Hero composition
  (browser windows, project cards, data nodes, the resolved "LIFECYCLE
  PIPELINE / CODE-BUILD-ITERATE-SHIP" panel) fade and scale in around the
  hero panel, settling into the clean layered composition already
  specified in MASTER_PRD.txt Section 6.
* Motion comes to rest; the overlay dissolves per the dissolve rules in
  ARCHITECTURE & PLACEMENT above, revealing the real homepage underneath,
  already in its resting/idle state.
* From this point on, standard Section 6 interactions apply (mouse
  influences camera, scroll changes composition, hover reveals info,
  click opens project preview).

Purpose: resolution — the bug is visibly fixed (red → green), the site has
"loaded", the workspace is calm and structured, and the visitor is now on
the real site.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
TIMING & BEHAVIOR RULES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
* REVISED: total sequence duration is approximately 8 seconds across
  Beats A-D (A: ~1.2s, B: ~1.8s, C: ~3s, D: ~2s). This was previously
  ~5.5s and played back feeling too fast/rushed in testing — 8 seconds is
  the corrected target. Implement the timeline to actually match these
  per-beat durations; do not compress them to make the sequence feel
  "snappier" than specified. Do not extend beyond ~9 seconds total.
* Plays in full on EVERY page load and every refresh (not once-per-session,
  not once-per-device — see ARCHITECTURE & PLACEMENT above). Do NOT loop
  the sequence repeatedly within a single load; it plays exactly once per
  load, then dissolves into the homepage.
* If `prefers-reduced-motion` is enabled, skip directly to the Beat D
  end-state with no animated transition (including skipping the Beat B
  glitch entirely) per MASTER_PRD.txt Section 22 and Section 31, then
  reveal the homepage immediately.
* If WebGL is unavailable, fall back per MASTER_PRD.txt Section 34 (2D
  version of the hero visual) — the fallback may use a simple CSS
  crossfade between a "scattered" and "organized" static illustration to
  approximate the same story beat, but this is optional, not required.
* While the intro overlay is visible, it MAY block interaction with the
  underlying page (this is expected and acceptable, since it is a loading
  screen) — but it must never hang indefinitely; if assets are not ready
  by the end of the ~8-9s sequence, dissolve into the homepage anyway
  rather than extending the overlay further.
* Once dissolved, the overlay is fully removed/hidden and the Hero
  section's own separate idle 3D experience (Section 6) takes over —
  there is no replay of Beats A-D inside the Hero itself.

================================================================================
2. TECHNICAL APPROACH
================================================================================

* Implement as a dedicated overlay component (e.g. `IntroOverlay` /
  `LoadingExperience`) rendered at the top of the app layout, `position:
  fixed`, full viewport, highest z-index — separate from and mounted
  ABOVE the `Hero` component and its own 3D scene. Do not build this by
  reusing or extending the Hero's canvas/component directly; they are two
  independent 3D scenes with two independent purposes (intro vs. ongoing
  Hero background).
* Use the existing stack already defined in MASTER_PRD.txt Section 27:
  Three.js / React Three Fiber for the scene, GSAP for timeline
  sequencing. Do not introduce a new animation or 3D library for this
  addendum.
* Structure the sequence as a single GSAP timeline driving: the Beat A
  layout-glimpse opacity, the Beat B glitch/fracture and hero-panel
  reveal, material/emissive color interpolation, camera position/shake,
  and the Beat D panel fade/scale-ins — rather than independently-timed
  animations per beat, to keep the transitions coherent.
* Keep the geometry/asset count restrained (per MASTER_PRD.txt Section 26
  Performance Requirements) — reuse the same fragment/panel meshes across
  all four beats; only their position, rotation, and color change.
* On dissolve, fully unmount the overlay's 3D scene/canvas (dispose of
  the WebGL context, stop its render loop) rather than hiding it with
  CSS — it must not keep consuming GPU/render cycles in the background
  once the homepage is revealed, since it plays again fresh on next load
  anyway.
* The Hero section's own 3D scene (MASTER_PRD.txt Section 6) starts fresh,
  independently, once revealed — respecting its own idle/interaction and
  render-loop rules already defined there.

================================================================================
3. VISUAL DEPTH & COLOR ADJUSTMENTS
================================================================================

These changes apply site-wide, not just to the Hero, and extend
MASTER_PRD.txt Section 20 (Visual Design Direction).

3.1 Background depth
* Replace flat single-color section backgrounds with a subtle radial
  gradient or vignette (e.g. from #111111 toward #0A0A0A) so sections
  read as having depth rather than a single flat plane.
* Add a very light film-grain / noise texture at low opacity across dark
  backgrounds to avoid a flat, synthetic look. Must remain subtle enough
  not to affect text legibility or contrast requirements (Section 31).

3.2 Accent color usage
* The accent color (#8AB4FF) is currently underused — mostly confined to
  small labels and numerals. Extend its use to:
  - A soft glow/halo behind key 3D elements in the Hero.
  - Active/hover states on navigation items (underline or glow, not a
    solid fill).
  - Hover state borders or glows on interactive cards (skills, projects).
* Keep usage restrained and intentional — this is still a single-accent
  system per Section 20; do not introduce additional accent colors.

3.3 Surface hierarchy
* Reinforce the existing three-tier surface system (Base #0A0A0A / Primary
  Surface #111111 / Secondary Surface #171717) by using it more
  consistently to separate foreground cards from their background section,
  rather than relying on borders alone.
